# Background
Wondering around the library today, one book took my attention. \
It was the **"Introduction to Programming in Java: An Interdisciplinary Approach" by Robert Sedgewick**. \
Huge around 800 pages long book. Looking at it, made me imagine going through it. 

I wondered about ***how many great insights*** are in there to get from such an experienced teacher and scientist? 
Even though I'm much beyond the introductory courses to programming,  how much insight could i gather? 

But then I though about how long it would take. How *artificial* would solving these little example problems feel.
How again, **I would be learning not from the feedback from the real world but from the comments of a teacher** 

And then i thought about **the alternative**. About **building projects that interest me**. About **creating and validating my knowledge as I go as opposed to amassing facts that I don't use, of importance that I don't get.**

## Thesis 
Two types of learning rely on these principles:
1. **Learning theory is a prerequisite to practice**
  - Memorizing and following a teacher. Trusting him on authority. Preparing yourself thoroughly before facing actual tasks.
2. **Learning theory is to supplement and improve practice**
  - Focus on building and learn theory on a need-to basis, validating different approaches by the collision with the real world with disregard to authority.
